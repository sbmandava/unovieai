FAQ · For buyers

Straight answers, first.

The questions plant, quality, security and finance teams ask us before a pilot, answered in the first sentence.

01Does any of our data leave the site?

No, not by default. AgentMesh runs air-gapped on your factory network, and raw signals, images and models stay on site. The only things that go out are the ones you choose, such as work orders to your maintenance system, over outbound-only connections.

02What happens when the internet or site link drops?

Nothing changes at the machine. Each AgentMesh pod decides locally and keeps running without a link. Findings for AgentNexus queue up and sync when the link returns.

03Is Unovie a safety system?

No. AgentMesh is never on the path that moves equipment and makes no SIL claim. It acts directly only on low-consequence outputs such as a reject gate or rework diverter (Lane A). Anything safety-related is an alarm and a request that your PLC, safety system and operators decide (Lane B).

04What hardware does it run on?

Unovie's custom-built hardware accelerators, engineered for agentic performance, low latency and high availability. The reference AgentMesh pod is four AMD Ryzen AI Max+ 395 servers in a 3U shelf; NVIDIA Jetson AGX Thor serves robotics and vision at the tool, and NVIDIA DGX Spark serves as the AI engineering lab.

05Do we have to buy the hardware?

No. Unovie supplies the hardware configured and tested. You can buy it outright or take it as a service with no upfront hardware cost.

06What does a pilot cost?

The site audit is at no charge: a 1–2 day visit on site, followed by written findings and a pilot plan. The 6-week paid pilot is $40–60K and is credited in full against a production site signed within 90 days.

07What do we get at the end of the pilot?

One domain kit running on your own data, a baseline ROI view, a measured latency report and a signed evidence pack of what the system did and why.

08How is production priced?

Per monitored asset and domain kit, never per seat or per camera. We quote production after the pilot, when we both know the asset count and the value.

09How fast can we reach production?

The target is about one quarter from the end of the pilot to a production site, because the pilot's playbook, connections and models carry straight over.

10Which systems does it connect to?

Sensors, robot controllers, vision stations, test rigs, PLCs (read-only, plus listed Lane A outputs), MES and quality systems, historians, and work-order systems such as SAP and Maximo. Industrial protocols include OPC UA, EtherCAT and Modbus.

11Is it certified or compliant with NIST, CMMC or IEC 62443?

Unovie is designed against NIST SP 800-53 and the NIST AI RMF and produces evidence for programs such as SP 800-171/CMMC and IEC 62443. Compliance is determined by your assessor, not by a product.

12Does the AI change itself over time?

Only through tests. Each night the Kiln proposes small playbook and skill-adapter changes and keeps them only if they beat what is running on held-out cases. Every change is signed, logged and reversible, and the base model never changes.

13Do you train on our data for other customers?

No. Models improve on your site, from your data, and nothing is shared back to Unovie or to other customers.

14Where are your engineers?

Unovie is headquartered in Austin, Texas, with engineers on site across Texas and serving customers nationally.

Case study · Device test automation · 12 months

One year, one platform: AI-driven certification for satellite set-top boxes.

A national satellite pay-TV provider asked us to replace manual set-top box testing with automation they could trust. In twelve months we designed, built and deployed an air-gapped, GPU-accelerated verification platform in their device lab. It presses real remote buttons, watches the real video output, judges what it sees and hands engineers an evidenced verdict. By the end of the year the whole QA team could test at once, the client was building its own vision models in-house, and every result could be read in plain English.

02 — The problem

A tester, a remote and a screen.

The lab certifies every firmware build for six product lines, each with several versions in test at once. The goal was never to review faster. It was to make exhaustive certification cheaper than sampling.

/manual

Manual and unrepeatable

Coverage was whatever one person could get through in a day, and no two runs were quite the same.

/brittle

Brittle when automated

Earlier tools compared screenshots pixel by pixel. A font tweak or a new promo tile broke the test, so most failures were the tool's fault.

/evidence

Verdicts without evidence

A note in a spreadsheet is a recollection. An annotated proof frame and a log start a different conversation with a developer.

/audio

No audio at scale

One capture stream showed sixteen boxes, but the audio path reached one box at a time, so audio wasn't verified at scale at all.

/copy

Moving data is the bottleneck

At terabits per second of decoded video, frames can't be copied through the CPU. Every step has to run in GPU memory.

/time

Failures happen over time

Freeze, macroblocking, stuck splash screens and audio dropouts only show across a sequence of frames.

/matrix

A matrix, not a checklist

Six product lines × several firmware versions: the same check has a different correct answer on each.

/physical

Physical control at scale

Every verdict starts with a real IR keypress. Thousands of remotes need scheduling, with hardware locked so jobs never collide.

04 — What we delivered

An engine, then a platform.

/engine

The verification engine

Drives the remote, reads the screen with detection and OCR, judges A/V health on the GPU and attaches proof frames and a written report to every verdict. Sixteen boxes per 4K stream, sliced on the GPU. Tests are configuration, not code.

19 of 19 featureson schedule
/factory

The model factory

An on-premise path from raw footage to labelled dataset to trained, versioned model. The client now retrains its own detectors: mAP rose from 0.836 to 0.961 in one cycle.

in-house modelsversioned
/channels

Adding a channel is a data task

Channels are matched against a managed library of reference images, so a new or rebranded channel needs a picture, not a retrain. The library rebuilds in under two seconds. Below a confidence margin the answer is “unknown” and a person looks.

no retraininghonest unknown
/scheduling

Lab-wide scheduling

The whole QA team tests at once across 8 equipment rooms, with hardware locked per job, per-user attribution, a full audit trail and inventory synced from the client's own configuration database.

no collisionsaudit trail
/language

A private language layer

Plain-English reports and plain-English test authoring, on separate inference hardware inside the lab. Facts such as channel numbers come from configuration, never from the model.

no cloudno made-up facts
/audio

Audio, from zero to sixteen

Networked control of the audio routing lets one scenario step cycle through every box and check measured signal level, so audio is certified on all sixteen paths.

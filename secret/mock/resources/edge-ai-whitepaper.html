Training Without Retraining: Custom Models on the Edge

When to change a model, when to leave it alone, and how to keep every change easy to undo on edge hardware.

Unovie.AI Full version available on request

Summary

Teams often try to fix a weak model by fine-tuning it. On edge hardware that is usually the wrong place to start. Memory is tight and data has to stay on site. Every change also has to be easy to undo. This paper explains when changing a model is worth it and when other options work better. It draws on recent research into how learning systems adapt and turns it into practical advice for edge teams.

Who it is for

Architects, ML engineers and platform leads who need custom AI models to run on site, on fixed hardware, with data that stays local.

What you will learn

  • Why fine-tuning is often the wrong first step on edge hardware.
  • How to tell if your model is too small or is simply missing context about your task.
  • How to test changes honestly, so nothing ships unless it does better on cases it has never seen.
  • What to look for in edge hardware for this kind of work.
  • How to decide when retraining the model really is the right call.

The full paper describes how Unovie does this in practice. We share it with engineering teams usually as part of a technical call.

Every controls engineer we meet asks the same first question: "Is your AI going to write to my PLC?"

No. A language model is never on the path that moves equipment.

AgentMesh sorts what it may do by consequence:

Lane A is for low-consequence outputs only, like diverting a part to rework, firing a reject gate or quarantining a device on the IT side. AgentMesh can act on these directly.

Lane B is anything that touches process safety. AgentMesh raises an alarm and writes a request. Your safety PLC, your cell controller and your operator decide.

Afterwards, it explains what happened and drafts the work order for someone to sign.

We are not a safety instrumented function and we make no SIL claim. Saying that plainly tends to make the safety review shorter.

A battery cell line and a battery storage fleet need very different things from AI.

On the line, speed matters. AgentMesh watches weld, formation and aging signals as cells, modules and packs are built. When a weld signature looks wrong, the part is diverted to rework. That&#x27;s a Lane A action: low consequence, so AgentMesh can take it directly, inside a 30 ms decision budget (target).

On a storage fleet, the stakes change. Early signs of thermal runaway go to the fire panel as an alarm, and that&#x27;s all AgentMesh does. The site&#x27;s own systems and the people on call decide what happens next.

It&#x27;s one domain kit with two lanes. Which lane a job belongs in is set when the kit is designed, not decided in the moment.

Every alarm and every diversion is written to a signed Evidence Log, so the quality team and the safety reviewer can see what the system saw and why it acted.

A robot joint rarely fails without warning. The warning is usually in torque data nobody had time to read.

AgentMesh reads robot controllers, servo drives and motion axes on every cell: joint current, torque, vibration and cycle data.

It compares each joint with its own history and asks two questions. Have we seen this pattern before? Did it end badly?

When a joint or gearbox starts to drift, two things happen. Parts from that cell can be diverted to rework in under 30 ms (target), a low-consequence Lane A action. And a work order with the evidence attached goes to maintenance, so the repair gets planned instead of forced by a stopped line.

Stopping the robot or changing its motion is never our call. That stays with the cell controller, the safety PLC and the operator.

Robot & Motion Health is one of our packaged agentic jobs, deployed in weeks.

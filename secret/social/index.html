A robot joint rarely fails without warning. The warning is usually in the torque data, weeks before the gearbox gives out.

Controllers already stop a robot on hard limits: a collision, overcurrent, overtemperature. Robot makers also offer their own monitoring, usually one brand at a time and often through the cloud. Slow wear on a mixed line still tends to show up as a stopped cell.

AgentMesh watches it on site. It reads joint current, torque and vibration from every controller and drive on the line, compares each joint with its own history, and flags a drifting joint within 30 ms (target). Parts from that cell go to rework, and maintenance gets a work order with the evidence attached. Stopping or changing the robot's motion stays with the cell controller, the safety PLC and the operator.

We're based in Austin. If your robots only speak up with a fault code, let us show you what the torque data already knows.
